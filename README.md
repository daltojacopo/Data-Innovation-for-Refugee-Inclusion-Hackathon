# Cashy household review prototype

A static dashboard mockup for the Cashy Oversight Challenge. Open `index.html` in a browser; the case data is loaded from `data/demo-cases.js`, so the prototype does not require a server or a database.

## Current sample data

- `data/S8.synthetic_cashy_sample.csv` is the challenge's 1,900-row synthetic S8 sample.
- The six displayed case records in `data/demo-cases.js` are examples selected from that synthetic sample and assigned fictional dashboard IDs. Factor scores and household attributes come from those sample rows.
- The reference values are the S8 sample medians for each of the eight scorecard factors. They are fixed for this first prototype stage; decisions recorded in the browser do not update the medians.
- The eligibility probabilities are illustrative interface values. They are not generated by a trained model and are not validated against `EligibilityTarget`.
- Interviewer notes and timestamped transcript excerpts are fictional examples written for the prototype. Their timestamps are illustrative; the S8 sample does not include interviewer free-text notes or interview recordings.
- The global importance, standard deviation, and p-values shown with factors are the supplied model-analysis statistics for `EligibilityTarget`. Importance is global predictive reliance, not a case-specific causal contribution or a directional effect.
- Administrative fields (`ScoreCOMAR_PIL`, `ScoreIntenciones`, `ScoreDuplicidad`) are displayed separately. The raw modifiers are retained to avoid assigning unsupported policy interpretations to their values.

Negative coping is shown only as a scorecard vulnerability factor. It does not establish misuse of cash or predict how a household will spend assistance. Eligibility remains the caseworker's decision and may depend on administrative checks and funding.

## Later prototype stage

The next planned iteration is to update the reference medians from all completed assessments, regardless of the operator's eligibility decision. Any such live or session-level reference will need a clear definition of the contributing case pool and should not imply that approved cases are representative of all households.
